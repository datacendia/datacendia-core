## How Datacendia Helps Napoli

### Scenario 1: Sell-High / Buy-Smart Transfer Model
**Decision Type:** `PlayerTransferDecision`
**Napoli's Problem:** Napoli's business model is unique: develop or scout undervalued talent, win, then sell at peak value and reinvest. After winning the 2023 Scudetto, Napoli sold Osimhen (€75M+), Kim Min-jae (€50M), Zieliński (free), and others totalling €200M+ — then scouted replacements at a fraction of the cost. Every AI valuation (sell price, replacement target value, comparable analysis) must be defensible to FIGC, UEFA FSR, and De Laurentiis's own investment thesis.
**Datacendia's Solution:** Captures sell-side valuations (market timing, comparable deals, agent fee analysis) and buy-side AI recommendations (undervaluation metrics, performance projections). CendiaPrecedent tracks whether the sell-high/buy-smart model consistently produces value. Evidence for FIGC and UEFA.
**Applicable Regulations:** FIGC financial rules, UEFA FSR, Italian corporate law

### Scenario 2: Owner-Operator Governance
**Decision Type:** `FinancialFairPlayDecision`
**Napoli's Problem:** De Laurentiis is both owner and day-to-day decision maker — no board separation. Italian corporate governance law and FIGC rules require demonstrable checks on owner decisions. If an AI model recommends against a transfer but the owner overrides it, that override needs documentation.
**Datacendia's Solution:** Override accountability captures when humans override AI recommendations — who, why, what risks were accepted. Critical for an owner-operator club.
**Applicable Regulations:** Italian corporate governance, FIGC, UEFA FSR

### Scenario 3: Stadio Maradona Operations (54,726)
