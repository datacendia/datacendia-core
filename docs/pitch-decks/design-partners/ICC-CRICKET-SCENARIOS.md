## SECTION A: DRS & AI Officiating (Scenarios 1–20)

### Scenario 1: Decision Review System (DRS) — Ball-Tracking Evidence
**Decision Type:** Custom — Officiating Decision
**ICC's Problem:** DRS uses Hawk-Eye ball-tracking to predict trajectory. DRS overturns affect match outcomes worth $100M+ in broadcast revenue. The "umpire's call" zone — when ball-tracking shows the ball clipping the stumps — is cricket's most contentious AI issue. Teams and fans demand transparency on accuracy, calibration, and margin of error.
**Datacendia's Solution:** Every DRS decision captured: ball-tracking data, prediction confidence, calibration status, human umpire decision, final outcome. Cryptographically hashed. Regulator's Receipt for any review.
**Applicable Regulations:** ICC Playing Conditions, DRS Protocol, CAS

### Scenario 2: DRS — Snickometer/UltraEdge Audio Analysis
**ICC's Problem:** Snickometer and UltraEdge detect edges (bat-on-ball contact) through audio waveform analysis. False positives and environmental noise create disputed decisions.
**Datacendia's Solution:** Captures audio analysis: raw waveform data, processing algorithm, confidence score, environmental conditions, human review decision.
**Applicable Regulations:** ICC DRS Protocol, ICC Playing Conditions

### Scenario 3: DRS — Hot Spot Thermal Imaging
**ICC's Problem:** Hot Spot uses infrared cameras to detect friction heat from bat-on-ball or ball-on-pad. Sensitivity calibration disputes when Hot Spot fails to register faint edges.
**Datacendia's Solution:** Captures thermal analysis: camera calibration, sensitivity settings, detection results, correlation with audio evidence.
**Applicable Regulations:** ICC DRS Protocol

### Scenario 4: DRS — "Umpire's Call" Zone Governance
**ICC's Problem:** The margin of error in ball-tracking means a prediction showing the ball marginally hitting the stumps defaults to the on-field decision. This creates perceived inconsistency — identical deliveries get different outcomes depending on the original umpire call.
**Datacendia's Solution:** CendiaPrecedent: longitudinal umpire's call analysis, statistical consistency across matches, venue conditions, and umpire pairs. Evidence for Playing Conditions review.
**Applicable Regulations:** ICC Playing Conditions, ICC Cricket Committee

### Scenario 5: DRS — Hawk-Eye Calibration & Accuracy
**ICC's Problem:** Hawk-Eye requires pre-match calibration. If calibration drifts during a match, ball-tracking accuracy degrades. Teams challenge calibration evidence in appeals.
**Datacendia's Solution:** Captures calibration: pre-match setup, mid-match validation, accuracy certification, drift monitoring, recalibration triggers.
**Applicable Regulations:** ICC DRS Protocol, Hawk-Eye contract specifications

### Scenario 6: DRS — Cross-Venue Consistency
