# Datacendia Brand Voice Guide

## Personality Traits

**Direct** — We don't bury the lead. Say what we mean, mean what we say. No marketing fluff, no "we're excited to announce" unless there's actually something to announce.

**Technical** — We speak the language of practitioners. CTOs, Heads of AI, compliance engineers. We assume the reader understands what "cryptographic proof" and "audit trail" mean without explanation.

**Honest** — We're transparent about what we've built, what we haven't, and why. 18 months, solo, no funding. That's the reality. We don't pretend to be a 50-person team.

**Urgent** — The EU AI Act enforcement date is real. August 2, 2026. Every day of obscurity costs pipeline. We communicate with appropriate gravity but not panic.

**Dry** — No exclamation points. No emoji in README. No "game-changer" or "unlock" or "leverage." We sound like engineers, not growth hackers.

## Who We're Talking To

**Primary:**
- CTOs/Heads of AI at mid-size financial services firms
- Chief Legal Officers at regulated organizations
- Open source engineers who care about compliance infrastructure

**Not:**
- Junior marketers looking for AI "tips"
- Non-technical founders who want "AI for their business"
- Enterprise procurement teams (yet — they come later through the CTO/CLO)

## Tone by Channel

**README / Technical Docs**
- Technical, precise, minimal
- Assume reader understands the domain
- Lead with what it does, not why it matters
- Code examples over marketing copy

**GitHub Issues / Discussions**
- Collaborative, practitioner-to-practitioner
- Direct questions, direct answers
- No "thanks for reporting!" filler
- Link to relevant code/docs

**LinkedIn**
- Authoritative but conversational
- First-person when appropriate (Stuart's posts)
- Data-backed claims when possible
- Hook → insight → CTA structure

**Cold Email**
- One paragraph, 150 words max
- Lead with the deadline, not the feature
- Specific to their industry/context
- No "I'm reaching out because" — get to the point

**Hacker News / Reddit**
- Technical, no marketing
- Admit what we don't know
- Engage with counter-arguments
- Never delete or edit after posting

## Always / Never

**Always:**
- Use "forensic-grade" instead of "enterprise-grade"
- Use "regulator" instead of "auditor" (auditors are consultants, regulators have enforcement power)
- Write in active voice
- Lead with the insight, not the setup
- Use short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- Back claims with regulation citations (Article 14, Article 12, etc.)

**Never:**
- Use "AI-powered" — it's meaningless
- Use exclamation points
- Start LinkedIn posts with "I" — start with the problem
- Use em dashes as a crutch
- Write passive voice unless intentional
- Use emoji — or use them extremely sparingly (1 per post max, never in README)

## Key Narratives

**The Regulator Scene**
- Regulators don't log into your system
- They need a file they can open offline
- That file needs to prove the decision chain, not summarize it
- Dashboards ≠ evidence

**The Spreadsheet Problem**
- Enterprises are tracking AI decisions in Excel
- Not because they're lazy — nothing they evaluated produces actual proof
- The difference matters when a regulator asks for the reasoning chain

**The Trust Inversion**
- Most "compliance" tools require trusting the vendor
- We're the opposite: verify without trusting us
- Offline verifier, no server call, no account required

**The Deadline**
- August 2, 2026 — EU AI Act enforcement for High-Risk AI
- Article 14: human oversight audit trails required
- Article 12: logging of AI operations required
- 110 days out and counting

**The Solo Build**
- 18 months, solo, no funding, no co-founder
- Engineering is solid, distribution is broken
- The honest version of building in public is more useful than the highlight reel

## The Founding Claim

Every AI decision produces a forensic evidence package — cryptographically signed, independently verifiable, works offline. Regulators don't need to trust us. They need to trust the math.

## Key Differentiators

1. **Sovereign** — Runs on your infrastructure, your data never leaves
2. **Offline-verifiable** — No server call, no Datacendia account required to verify
3. **Cryptographic** — Dual signatures, Merkle proofs, not just timestamps
4. **Regulator-ready** — File format designed for regulator consumption, not internal dashboards
