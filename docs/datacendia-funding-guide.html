5. NSF SBIR Phase I

$275K (6 months) Strong Fit

Why Datacendia Qualifies

NSF funds breakthrough technology innovation. Datacendia's multi-agent deliberation with cryptographic audit trail, sovereign architecture patterns (data diode, TPM attestation, federated mesh), and AI governance is novel R&D.

Eligibility Requirements

  • US small business (≤500 employees) — ✅ Datacendia, LLC
  • At least 51% US-owned — verify this is met
  • PI (Principal Investigator) must be primarily employed by Datacendia
  • Novel technology with commercial potential

Step-by-Step Application

Step 1 — Register on Research.gov

Go to research.gov. Create an account and register Datacendia as an organization. Also register on SAM.gov (required for all federal grants — takes 2–4 weeks).

Step 2 — Get a UEI Number

Obtain a Unique Entity Identifier from SAM.gov. This replaces the old DUNS number. Required before submission.

Step 3 — Review NSF SBIR Topics

Go to seedfund.nsf.gov. Review open topics. Best fits for Datacendia:
• AI/ML (AI) — Multi-agent systems, explainable AI
• Smart & Connected Communities (SC) — Decision intelligence infrastructure
• Cybersecurity (CY) — Cryptographic audit, zero-trust architecture

Step 4 — Submit Project Pitch (2-page pre-screen)

NSF SBIR requires a Project Pitch first (2 pages). This is a quick pre-screen. Include:
• Technical Innovation: Multi-agent AI deliberation with structured dissent and cryptographic provenance
• Commercial Potential: $47B Decision Intelligence TAM, enterprise contracts $50K–$1.5M+
• Technical Objectives: Sovereign deployment patterns, zero-copy architecture, AI governance framework

Step 5 — Receive Pitch Invitation (or Decline)

NSF responds to pitches within 3 weeks with "Invite" or "Decline." If invited, you submit a full proposal.

Step 6 — Write Full Proposal (15 pages)

Key sections:
• Project Description (15 pages max): Technical plan, innovation, R&D approach
• Budget & Budget Justification: Personnel, equipment, cloud compute, travel
• Biographical Sketch: PI's qualifications
• Current & Pending Support: Other grants/funding
• Data Management Plan: How research data will be managed
• Commercialization Plan: Path to market, customers, revenue model

Step 7 — Submit via Research.gov

Submit the full proposal through Research.gov's SBIR portal. Rolling deadlines (check current cycle).

Step 8 — Panel Review & Decision (3–6 months)

NSF convenes a review panel. If awarded, receive $275K for 6-month Phase I. Phase II ($1M, 2 years) follows successful Phase I.

Datacendia Proposal Framing

  • Title: "Sovereign Multi-Agent Decision Intelligence with Cryptographic Provenance"
  • Technical Innovation: 14-agent AI council with formal dissent mechanisms, deterministic replay, and Ed25519/RSA-PSS cryptographic audit trail
  • Broader Impacts: Transparent AI governance for critical infrastructure, healthcare, defense, and regulated industries
  • Commercial Plan: $50K–$1.5M enterprise contracts, 4 deployment modes including air-gapped sovereign
Important: SAM.gov registration takes 2–4 weeks. Start this immediately even before writing the proposal. You cannot submit without it.
Pro Tip: NSF SBIR has a ~20% acceptance rate for Phase I. The key differentiator is demonstrating true technical novelty (not just another AI chatbot) and clear commercial path. Datacendia's cryptographic audit trail and sovereign architecture patterns are highly novel.

6. NSF I-Corps

$50K Strong Fit

Why Datacendia Qualifies

I-Corps is a customer discovery program. It's also the gateway to SBIR. Completing I-Corps significantly improves SBIR acceptance rates and gives you $50K for customer discovery interviews.

Step-by-Step Application

Step 1 — Form Your Team

I-Corps requires a 3-person team:
• Entrepreneurial Lead (EL): Founder/CEO
• Technical Lead (TL): CTO or lead engineer
• I-Corps Mentor: External mentor with industry experience (I-Corps can help match you)

Step 2 — Apply Through a Regional Hub

Visit icorps.nsf.gov. Find your regional I-Corps Hub. Apply to their cohort program.

Step 3 — Complete the Application

Describe your technology hypothesis and target customer segment. For Datacendia: "Enterprise decision-makers in regulated industries need sovereign AI deliberation with cryptographic evidence."

Step 4 — Attend 7-Week Program

If accepted, complete the 7-week intensive: 100+ customer interviews, weekly cohort sessions, business model iteration.

Step 5 — Deliver Final Presentation

Present findings: validated customer segments, pricing validation, go-to-market insights.

What You Get

  • $50K grant for customer discovery (travel, interviews, research)
  • Structured methodology for validating product-market fit
  • Priority consideration for NSF SBIR proposals — this is the biggest value
  • Network of mentors and fellow founders
Pro Tip: I-Corps graduates have significantly higher SBIR acceptance rates (some reports suggest 2–3x). Do I-Corps first, then SBIR. The $50K funds 100+ customer interviews that also validate your sales pitch.

7. DARPA SBIR/STTR

$50K–$2M+ Strong Fit

Why Datacendia Qualifies

DARPA funds breakthrough defense technology. Datacendia's air-gapped deployment, TPM attestation, data diode patterns, and multi-agent decision support are directly relevant to DoD decision-making needs.

Step-by-Step Application

Step 1 — Monitor DARPA SBIR Topics

Visit darpa.mil/work-with-us/for-small-businesses and dodsbirsttr.mil. DARPA releases topic solicitations periodically. Look for topics under:
• I2O (Information Innovation Office) — AI, ML, decision support
• DSO (Defense Sciences Office) — Complex systems, decision-making under uncertainty

Step 2 — Register on DSIP

Create an account on the DoD SBIR/STTR Innovation Portal (DSIP) at dodsbirsttr.mil. Complete company registration.

Step 3 — Ensure SAM.gov Registration

Same SAM.gov and UEI registration as NSF SBIR. If already done for NSF, you're set.

Step 4 — Write Phase I Proposal

DARPA Phase I proposals are typically 20–25 pages. Key sections:
• Technical Volume: Describe multi-agent deliberation, cryptographic provenance, sovereign deployment
• Cost Volume: Budget for 6-month Phase I
• Company Information: Team qualifications, facilities
Frame Datacendia as: "Sovereign AI Decision Support System for Contested Environments"

Step 5 — Submit via DSIP

Submit through the DSIP portal before the topic deadline. Deadlines are firm.

Step 6 — Evaluation (2–4 months)

DARPA evaluates on: technical merit, team qualifications, cost reasonableness, and relevance to defense needs.

Datacendia Defense Framing

  • Air-gapped deployment → SIPR/JWICS classified network operation
  • TPM attestation → Hardware root of trust for military systems
  • Data diode patterns → One-way data flow for cross-domain solutions
  • Multi-agent deliberation → Tactical decision support with adversarial challenge
  • Cryptographic audit trail → Chain of custody for decision accountability
  • Deterministic replay → After-action review for decision forensics
Pro Tip: DARPA Phase I is $50K–$250K. Phase II is $500K–$2M. DARPA also has "Direct to Phase II" for companies that can demonstrate Phase I-equivalent work has already been done. Datacendia's existing platform could qualify for Direct to Phase II.
