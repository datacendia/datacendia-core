Are You AI Act Ready?

The EU AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689) is the world's first comprehensive AI regulation. High-risk AI system enforcement begins August 2, 2026. If your organization deploys AI for credit scoring, insurance pricing, legal research, hiring, or any Annex III high-risk use case — you must demonstrate compliance or face penalties up to 7% of global annual turnover.

What the AI Act Requires (Articles 9–15)

  • 1
    Risk Management System (Art. 9) — Continuous identification, analysis, and mitigation of AI risks throughout the system lifecycle.
  • 2
    Data Governance (Art. 10) — Training, validation, and testing datasets must meet quality criteria. Bias examination required.
  • 3
    Technical Documentation (Art. 11) — Complete documentation of system design, development, and intended purpose before market placement.
  • 4
    Record-Keeping & Logging (Art. 12) — Automatic logging of events for traceability. Logs must be retained for the system's lifetime or a minimum period set by sector law.
  • 5
    Transparency & User Information (Art. 13) — Deployers must be informed of system capabilities, limitations, and intended use.
  • 6
    Human Oversight (Art. 14) — Systems must allow effective human oversight, including the ability to override or stop the AI system.
  • 7
    Accuracy, Robustness & Cybersecurity (Art. 15) — Appropriate levels of accuracy, resilience to errors, and security against adversarial attacks.

Non-Compliance Penalties (Art. 99)

Applicable to providers and deployers of high-risk AI systems in the EU market.

Most Organizations Are Not Ready

Based on our conversations with EU financial institutions and law firms, the most common gaps are:

  • ✗
    No audit trail for AI decisions — AI outputs are used but not logged with the specificity Art. 12 requires.
  • ✗
    Human oversight is informal — An employee "reviews" AI output but there's no documented, timestamped supervision chain.
  • ✗
    No risk management lifecycle — Risk assessments were done once at procurement, not continuously as Art. 9 requires.
  • ✗
    Technical documentation is incomplete — Model cards exist but don't cover the full Art. 11 requirements for system-level documentation.
